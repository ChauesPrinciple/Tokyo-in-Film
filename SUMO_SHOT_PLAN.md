# SUMO SHOT PLAN — COMPOSITION-FIRST

**Client:** Sumo wrestler (the subject) is the paying participant. This is not a broadcast of matches. It is a **portrait of a day at sumo told from inside the experience** — the wrestler's hand, his friend's eye, the venue's geometry, the ritual's detail. Matches are the spine, not the whole body.

**Duration:** 3 hours continuous. Ryogoku Kokugikan or equivalent.

---

## CAMERA ROLES — COMPOSITIONAL DIVISION OF LABOR

| Camera | Compositional Job | Frame Language |
|---|---|---|
| **XT2** | The **anchor frame**. Never moves. Establishes scale, time, truth. | Reframed in post from 360° — wide symmetric, dead-center dohyo, proscenium composition |
| **X5** | The **god's-eye geometry**. Reveals physics, vectors, ritual symmetry. | Top-down circle — radial composition, mandala framing, reframeable tracking |
| **GO Ultra** | The **first-person thread**. Subjective, imperfect, human. Lives on the client's wrist. | POV eye-level, hand-led, natural tilt and pan. Reframed tight — never fisheye delivery |
| **Ace Pro 2** | The **author's eye**. Deliberate composition, the shots you compose with intent. | Rule of thirds, short lens for ritual intimacy, 4K/120 for impact slow-mo |

**Compositional facts to exploit:**
- The **tsuriyane** (hanging roof) is a natural **top frame** — use it to cap wide shots.
- The **dohyo is raised ~60cm** — shooting from floor level gives **low-angle heroic frame** on wrestlers against the roof.
- Four **shimenawa tassels** at the roof corners = natural **third-point anchors** for overhead or wide-angle frames.
- The **hanamichi runway** gives a **deep one-point perspective** corridor.
- **Shinpan in black kimono** at four corners = **compositional anchors**, dark mass against lit dohyo.
- **Gyoji in saturated silk** = the only intense color on the dohyo — always a **focal magnet**, compose around him.

## XT2 — THE ANCHOR FRAME

**Physical setup:** Locked, tripod or clamp. Position: front row **shomen-men** (east side, facing the shimpan-cho), **centered on the dohyo's north-south axis**, lens ~1.2m off the ground so the dohyo surface reads as a plane, not foreshortened into a line.

**Why there:** Every bout's vector is legible. Tachiai from left-right, push-outs readable, gyoji framed symmetrically, tsuriyane caps the top, four shimpan at the thirds.

**Reframe outputs from the 360° sphere (post):**

| Reframe | FOV | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Full proscenium** | ~100° | Master shot of every bout — tsuriyane top, tawara bottom, shimpan left/right thirds |
| **Dohyo-only** | ~60° | Tight on the fight circle — wrestlers fill horizontal thirds |
| **Crowd reverse** | ~120° behind | Audience reaction — use in cuts for catharsis moments |
| **Time-lapse pull** | Hemisphere | Arena filling and emptying — one shot, whole day |

**Compositional discipline:** Never reframe the XT2 to move during a bout. This camera is **static truth**. Only cut to its reframes; never animate them. That stillness is the value.

## X5 — GOD'S-EYE GEOMETRY

**Physical setup:** Telescoping pole clamped to a balcony rail or the shomen press-box lip, extended over the dohyo center. Lens plane **parallel to the dohyo surface**. 8K so reframe pulls stay clean.

**The four compositional payloads only this camera gives you:**

1. **Chiri-chōzu mirror symmetry.** Wrestlers enter east and west, squat at the tawara edges, clap — perfect **bilateral symmetry around the dohyo's center axis**. Reframe to a square 1:1 crop with wrestlers at the left and right vertical thirds, gyoji tassel dead center.

2. **Tachiai collision vector.** At impact, pull the overhead into a **tight circle crop**. Two bodies compress from the outside of frame to the center. Who gets lower is visible in the body mass above the line; who gets inside position is visible in arm geometry. This is the only angle where **sumo physics is a diagram**.

3. **Grip-fight radial drift.** During long bouts, wrestlers circle. Overhead shows **the center of mass drifting** — a push-out is a **radial vector from center to tawara**. Reframe with the center of the dohyo at the frame's center point; the loser's exit line is a clean ray from center to edge.

4. **Gyoji + wrestlers triad.** Gyoji in silk + two wrestlers = a **three-body composition** that rotates during the bout. Reframe to include the gyoji — you get a **moving triangle** that collapses at decision.

**What NOT to do with the X5:** Don't deliver fisheye. Don't leave it wide. Every X5 clip in the edit should be a **reframed, roughly 40–70° crop** of the sphere, treated like a locked overhead camera that sometimes pans by reframing.

**Dohyo-iri overhead (the hero shot):** When 16 makuuchi wrestlers enter the ring in kesho-mawashi and form a circle, the X5 captures a **perfect mandala** — 16 bodies at equal angular spacing, embroidered aprons radiating inward. Hold on this wide, pull slowly tighter. One of the two or three shots the whole film is built around.

## GO ULTRA — THE CLIENT'S WRIST (FIRST-PERSON THREAD)

**Mount:** Back of the client's non-dominant hand, magnetic. Lens pointed **forward off the knuckle line**, so when his hand is at rest the camera is level.

**The compositional rule for every GO Ultra shot:** Frame should feel like **his visual attention**, not like coverage. That means:
- Unsteady is good — don't over-stabilize in post.
- Tilt follows his head tilt.
- What he looks at goes to the **center of the reframed crop**, because that's what eyes do.
- Reframe to roughly **55–70° FOV** for delivery — wider than a phone, narrower than action-cam default. This is close to natural foveal-plus-peripheral awareness.

**The compositional beats he creates just by existing:**

- **Entry corridor, low angle.** His hand at hip height entering the kokugikan. The wooden corridor becomes a **one-point perspective tunnel**, his hand in the lower third, architecture vanishing to the upper-center third. Reframe can widen here to emphasize the venue scale.
- **Seat arrival, the reveal.** Hand on the railing as he takes his seat. First frame is **railing low third, dohyo upper third** — the entire film's subject is introduced as a **horizon line** across his knuckles.
- **Program in lap.** Hand holds program. Program fills lower two thirds, his knees frame vertical left and right, dohyo visible in the **top third sliver**. This is a recurring **rest composition** — return to it between bouts.
- **The point.** He extends his arm to point at a wrestler. Camera moves with the arm — the **wrestler enters frame from the direction his hand indicates**. Reframe holds on whoever he pointed at. Do this during dohyo-iri specifically: one point per wrestler he wants the audience to notice.
- **The bento.** Hand picks up chopsticks, lifts food. This is a **macro-adjacent close-up** by accident — texture of rice, fish, pickles. Reframe tight, 40° FOV, shallow-DOF-looking result.
- **The clap.** When he claps, both hands enter frame. **Symmetrical composition** at the moment of collective applause. Reframe square 1:1 for this — it reads as a ritual moment.
- **The standing ovation.** He stands. Camera rises with his body. **Vertical whip** — other standing bodies enter the foreground thirds. Reframe keeps the dohyo locked in the upper center.

**Fixed placements (30–60 sec each, between bouts during shikiri windows):**

| Placement | Composition |
|---|---|
| **Tawara edge, east side** | Camera sits ON the straw bale. Frame: dohyo surface as a **receding plane** to the shikiri lines. A push-out in this frame means a body falls **toward the lens**. |
| **Salt basket rim** | Camera inside the rim, looking up. Wrestler's hand enters from frame-top, scoops salt, exits upward. **Negative space** below. The frame is a well. |
| **Shikiri line, low** | Camera between the two shikiri lines, ground level. Both wrestlers squat and their faces enter frame from opposite sides at their eye level. **Symmetric two-shot** with 5cm of air between their foreheads at glare-down. |
| **Gyoji's feet** | Camera at the south edge, 10cm off the dohyo. Gyoji's tabi-sock feet and silk hem in lower half; wrestlers' feet and the shikiri line in upper half. When gyoji raises gunbai, the fan enters from off-top. |

Each placement is **one bout only**. Pull the camera during the cleanup between bouts. Don't let placement shots become standard coverage — they're punctuation.

## ACE PRO 2 — THE AUTHOR'S EYE

**This is the only camera making real compositional decisions live.** Every other camera is locked, overhead, or wrist-attached. The Ace Pro 2 is the one where you think like a DP.

**Framing defaults:**
- **8K/30 for everything storytelling.** You will crop in post — 8K gives you a second reframe on top of your live composition.
- **4K/120 for three things only:** salt throw, tachiai impact, winner's exhale at the end of a bout. Slow-motion is a tool, not a mode.
- **Hold every composed shot for 8 seconds minimum.** You can cut a two-second beat in post; you can't stretch a two-second shot to hold.

**Specific compositions to pursue:**

### Ritual-intimacy frames (rule of thirds, tight)

- **Salt throw, low-angle behind wrestler.** Place yourself on the east side floor at the tawara. Wrestler's back fills the left third, salt arcs across the upper-center third against the dark shinpan uniforms on the opposite side. Salt is the bright element — the entire composition is **dark mass left, dark mass back, bright arc across top**.
- **Mawashi knot, macro.** Get close (permissible between bouts near the hanamichi). Silk weave, sweat, the knot at the back. Frame **fills 100% with texture.** No negative space. This is the only shot that should look this way in the whole film.
- **Feet on clay.** Low angle, dohyo surface in lower third, feet and calves in middle, mawashi hem and rope as the top frame line. Clay dust on skin. Hold as wrestler shifts weight pre-tachiai.
- **Gyoji's gunbai hand.** The hand gripping the fan's handle. Silk sleeve fills frame left, fan handle is the leading line to off-frame-right where the decision happens. **Frame before the decision, hold through, cut at the call.**

### Hanamichi frames (deep perspective)

- **Arrival, wide long lens from end of hanamichi.** Wrestler walks toward camera from the shitaku-beya (dressing room). **One-point perspective** — vanishing point is the dressing room door, wrestler fills the center third growing across 6-8 seconds. Don't rack; let him walk into his own size.
- **Arrival, tight, 40mm equivalent.** Same walk, but lens tight. **Shallow DOF look** via Ace Pro 2's sensor. Face lands in focus as he passes a distance threshold. Only for the client's bout and maybe one marquee bout.
- **Departure after loss.** Hanamichi from behind. Wrestler's back in center frame, crowd heads in the lower quarter at the edges. **Diminishing figure composition** — he gets smaller, the corridor swallows him. Hold to absurdity — 15 seconds. This is a grief shot.
- **Departure after win.** Same geometry, but cut short at 4 seconds. Winners don't get the long hold.

### Crowd composition frames

- **Balcony reverse, upper deck.** From the balcony, shoot back across the arena so the **dohyo is the vanishing point** and crowd heads are the foreground. Use for scale.
- **Single face reactions, tight.** Older man closing his eyes at a call. Child watching bewildered. Woman laughing at a mawari-keiko-style moment. These are **portraits**, not reactions — compose them like portraits: eyes on the upper third, clean background.
- **Hands — clapping, holding program, raising phone.** Isolate. Shallow frame. These become cutaways that carry ritual feeling without explanation.

### Client-specific (he is the paying subject)

- **In shitaku-beya if permitted.** Preparing the mawashi, water, stretching. **Available-light, window-lit portraiture.** Place him at the right vertical third, window light entering frame left. Hold on his face during stillness.
- **Walking the hanamichi, his bout.** Track from behind, then cut to locked wide on the dohyo for his entrance. Client gets the **reverse-angle treatment** — both back-of-walk AND arrival-to-camera coverage.
- **His bout, post-decision.** Whatever happens — win or loss — cut to Ace Pro 2 **tight on his face**, 85–100mm equivalent feel (crop from 8K). Hold for 6 seconds minimum. No music over this.
- **Post-match, quiet moment.** Sitting, water, breath. **Window-light portrait** again if possible. This is the film's landing.

| Time | Phase | XT2 | X5 | GO Ultra (client) | Ace Pro 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Arrival & venue | Time-lapse fill | Off (deploy) | Corridor POV, seat reveal, program-in-lap rest | Exterior wide, signage, crowd portraits |
| 0:20–0:40 | Pre-ceremonies | Locked wide | Deploy overhead | Spectator rest compositions | Shitaku-beya if permitted, mawashi macros, hanamichi deep-perspective |
| 0:40–1:10 | Juryo bouts (lower division) | Bout masters | Tachiai geometry on every bout | 2 fixed placements (tawara + salt basket), spectator reactions | Salt-throw slow-mo, gyoji hand, low-angle feet |
| 1:10–1:30 | Division break | Locked wide (arena movement) | Pause, reposition/charge | Bento, bathroom walk POV, program study | Crowd portraits, architecture details |
| 1:30–1:45 | **Dohyo-iri (hero sequence)** | Full ceremony wide | **Mandala overhead — the signature shot** | **Point sequence** — name each wrestler for the camera | Kesho-mawashi gold-thread macro, shiko slow-mo |
| 1:45–2:45 | Makuuchi bouts | Bout masters (including client's bout) | Every tachiai, every push-out vector | 2 more fixed placements (shikiri + gyoji feet), standing applause, the clap | Client hanamichi arrival, impact slow-mo, decision-moment gunbai, winner/loser face holds |
| 2:45–3:00 | **Client's bout + aftermath** | Locked (truth frame) | Overhead (geometry of his specific fight) | His own wrist during his walk out — he films his own arrival | **Client portrait series** — arrival, bout wide, post-match face hold, quiet moment |
| 3:00–3:15 | Departure | Emptying time-lapse | Off (wrap) | Exit corridor POV, last look back at dohyo | Exterior magic hour, signage close |

**Note on the client's own bout:** This is the film's peak. All four cameras converge. The XT2 gives the truth, the X5 gives the physics, the GO Ultra gives his own perspective entering and leaving, the Ace Pro 2 gives the face hold that the film is ultimately about.

## THE THREE SHOTS THE FILM IS BUILT AROUND

Every other shot supports these:

1. **X5 overhead mandala of the dohyo-iri.** 16 wrestlers in kesho-mawashi, radial symmetry, held for 8+ seconds.
2. **Ace Pro 2 post-match face hold on the client.** 6+ seconds, no music, available light, whatever emotion is there is the emotion.
3. **GO Ultra wrist-POV of the point.** His arm extends, the wrestler he names enters frame, the camera moves because he moved. The subjectivity of the whole film in one gesture.

If you come home with those three, the film exists. Everything else is texture, context, and connective tissue.

## POWER (brief — you have external chargers)

Run the XT2 and GO Ultra on external USB-C power banks with short hidden cable runs (wrist-to-pocket for GO Ultra; tripod-base-to-bag for XT2). Hot-swap the X5 once during the juryo→makuuchi break using a wall charger. Ace Pro 2 uses one internal battery through juryo, swap during the break, second internal through makuuchi. Don't overthink it — the cameras run, the shot plan doesn't.
