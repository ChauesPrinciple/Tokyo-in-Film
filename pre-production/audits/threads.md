# Setup/Payoff Thread Audit — LONGSHOT.txt

**Audit scope:** every planted element (object, behavior, line, claim) and its payoff trajectory. Dead threads and orphans flagged.

---

## Thread status key

- **CLEAN** — setup and payoff both present, connection legible.
- **PARTIAL** — setup present, payoff weak or incomplete.
- **DEAD** — setup present, no payoff. Must be cut or paid off.
- **ORPHAN** — payoff present without prior setup. Must be set up or cut.

---

## Act 1 setups and their trajectories

### SETUP-1: Nakamura's archive file (three years old, 03:47 timestamp, unopened)

- **Planted:** L96.
- **Reinforced:** L237 "The file from three years ago unopened."
- **Tanaka at dinner in Japanese:** L254–266 "The file. What about it. Open it." — confirms Tanaka knows.
- **Act 2 revelation:** L703 "Your archive. The file from three years ago. Look at it again."
- **Dream payoff:** L788 "Nakamura: the archive file. On her screen. Open. She is reading what she did not write. The handwriting is her own."
- **Status:** **PARTIAL.**
- **Problem:** The file's content is never revealed on screen or named in dialogue. The audience knows Nakamura has it, Tanaka knows she has it, both of them treat it as load-bearing — but we never learn what is IN it. "Reading what she did not write" is in the dream, which is interior, not diegetic. The thread generates mystery without ever landing.
- **Required:** either an on-screen reveal of the file's content (what was in it — another unfinished proof? a paper Nakamura was pressured to abandon? a prediction that matches the sector-nine data?), or cut the file entirely.
- **Recommendation:** Pay it off. The file should contain something that mathematically parallels Ito's proof — Nakamura's independent convergence on the same conclusion three years before launch, which she abandoned (suppressed herself, the way Ito's community suppressed him). This explains why Babel put her on the ship (she already had the framework, like Tanaka had Ito's papers) and pays off "it's what we'd already almost reached." One scene, 1–2 pages: Nakamura finally opens the file in Act 2 after Tanaka's confrontation. Audience sees the handwriting. Audience understands she has been carrying her own Ito the whole time.

### SETUP-2: Kanzashi pin in Nakamura's hair

- **Planted:** L94.
- **Later appearances:** none.
- **Status:** **DEAD.**
- **Problem:** An unusual personal object is planted ("has no business on a research vessel" was the cut tell) and then forgotten. It reads as flavor, not setup — but the script's own calling-out of its oddness frames it as a planted thread.
- **Required:** pay it off in one beat, or cut.
- **Options:** (a) cut the reference at L94, replace with neutral description of her hair; (b) pay off — e.g., during her Act 3 work at the console she pulls the kanzashi pin out and sets it on the desk the way Marshall sets his watch down; (c) Tanaka at some point notices it, which means something specific to them as two Japanese crew members that bridges the silence between them.
- **Recommendation:** option (b) — silent payoff at the relay-destruction planning scene. Costs nothing, closes the thread, deepens her character without dialogue.

### SETUP-11: Sixth empty sealed sleeping pod

- **Planted:** L339 — "Six sleeping pods. One empty and sealed."
- **Later appearances:** none.
- **Status:** **DEAD.**
- **Problem:** An empty sealed pod on a five-person mission invites questions. Why six pods? Who was the sixth person? Why was their pod sealed? The audience will read this as planted.
- **Required:** pay off or cut.
- **Recommendation:** This is a rich thread that could pay off MARSHALL's Meridian history. The sixth pod was Ito's — Babel originally planned a six-person crew with Ito as senior theorist, and his death left the pod. Or the sixth pod was intended for a mission psychologist who washed out and Carter replaced him. Or the pod is a cold-storage reserve for a casualty the mission is budgeted to expect. **Decide what it is, or cut the reference.**

### SETUP-19: Personal trauma kit

- **Planted:** L993 — "The personal trauma kit every crew member was issued at launch." FIRST MENTION AT USE.
- **Status:** **ORPHAN.**
- **Problem:** The trauma kit is used by Costa for suicide. The audience learns the kit exists at the moment it is used. No prior establishment. This violates show-not-tell and feels like a writer-convenience.
- **Required:** plant the trauma kit earlier. Options: Carter references "personal trauma kits" during the cold-open pre-launch calibration sequence (one line of dialogue, e.g., he signs off on kits being stocked). Or at dinner we see someone opening theirs for something mundane (a paracetamol tablet) and the presence of sedatives in the blister sheet is visible as background. Or Marshall's checklist includes "crew kits" as one of the items he ticks in the calibration room.
- **Recommendation:** **Add one line to the Day Cycle 01 calibration sequence.** Marshall's paper checklist could include "Personal trauma kits: stocked." Single line during L48–L61. Then when Costa opens hers in Act 2, it reads as something the audience has seen provisioned.

### SETUP-20: Dreams (first, second, and the missing third)

- **First dream:** L503 — shared, unacknowledged.
- **Second dream:** L771 — individualized. Each crew member's different.
- **Third dream:** production notes (from legacy script) promised this to Nakamura ("door fully open, something seen through it, she wakes screaming"). **NOT IN CURRENT SCRIPT.**
- **Status:** **PARTIAL / BROKEN.** The first two dreams are planted; the third was promised by the film's own design but does not appear.
- **Problem:** Act 3 has Nakamura calm, collected, working the relay-destruction solution. There is no dream beat that escalates the horror to a peak before the climactic action. The current script's Act 3 is all problem-solving.
- **Required:** decide. Either (a) write the third dream — Nakamura alone, the door fully open, something seen through it, she wakes and refuses to document — placed between the Day Cycle 61 Tanaka-corridor scene and the Day Cycle 62 planning scene. Or (b) cut the dream motif entirely (too expensive for payoff if we're not going to escalate). Or (c) leave it at two dreams and accept the thread is closed at the second.
- **Recommendation:** **Option (a).** Add the third dream. Nakamura alone. Thirty seconds of screen time. It motivates her action more strongly than "we drift, forty hours, no choice." It also pays off the "something the size of a door" visual from the first dream.
