## Pre-Production Phase

### 5/13: Logline + Theme Through Form

After Quiz 1. Watch in class: *Automation* (Japanese A.I. Robot Short Film, 13 min).

**Part 1: Logline**

Write one sentence: A [protagonist] must [goal], but [obstacle] stands in the way, or else [stakes]. Max 35 words. Specific, visible actions only. No vague language ("discovers who they are," "goes on a journey").

**Part 2: Theme Through Form**

In two or three sentences: What idea does the film suggest beyond its plot? Name one abstract concept (obsolescence, substitution, loyalty, memory). Then identify one cinematic technique that supported it at a specific moment: framing, composition, lighting, or sound. Reference what you saw, not what you assumed.

**Part 3: Catharsis in Automation**

The Catharsis assignment asks you to map a film's structure onto three stages: the Inhale (what the film makes you wait for and how pressure mounts), the Withholding (what is kept just out of reach and why), and the Exhale (whether the release is earned, and what you feel at the end).

Apply that framework to *Automation* now, while the film is fresh. In three or four sentences total: What does the film make you wait for? What is withheld and for how long? Does the ending release that pressure or deny it? When you write your Catharsis analysis on *13 Assassins*, *Kubi*, *Godzilla Minus One*, or *Chainsaw Man: Reze Arc*, this is the exact structure you will use at 500 words. Practice it here first at four sentences.

Post all three parts to the Stream.

### 5/14: Framing Redesign

After Quiz 2. Use a paused frame from *Chainsaw Man: Reze Arc*, specifically a two-character scene from the Kanda sequences.

Draw the existing frame in your notebook. Note where the subjects are placed, what shape they form, what the lighting is doing, and whether the composition is symmetrical or asymmetrical.

Redesign the shot using at least one of the following:

- Apply the rule of thirds: move the dominant subject to a power position off-center.
- Change the triangle: stable base-down communicates authority; inverted communicates instability.
- Shift from symmetrical to asymmetrical balance, or reverse it.
- Add negative space behind or around one character to signal isolation.

In two or three sentences, explain how your new composition changes what we read about the power dynamic. If the lighting logic changes with your redesign, name that too.

Then: sketch the opening frame of one scene from your own film. Apply at least one of the same compositional choices and explain what you want it to communicate. This sketch goes in your Storyboard deliverable.

Model a written response before students start. "His placement at the center of a symmetrical frame communicates control. In my redesign I pushed him to the left edge against empty street, which makes him look like he is about to disappear from the scene." Vague responses ("it changes the mood") are not acceptable.

Post the redesigned sketch, the written response, and the opening frame sketch to the Stream.

## Pre-Production: Excursion Preparation

### 5/18: Reze Arc Excursion Prep

No quiz today. Students are planning the specific shots they intend to capture at the real Kanda filming locations from *Chainsaw Man: Reze Arc*. This is not general practice. It is the in-class draft of the Pilgrimage Shots Location Plan deliverable.

**Part A: Location Research and Floor Plan**

Each student or group selects one specific scene from *Reze Arc* set in Kanda. Find the real-world location and confirm it now. Draw a bird's-eye floor plan showing subject positions, camera placement, light sources (account for Kanda's street lighting and the time of day you plan to shoot), and how action moves through the space. You need a Google Maps pin before the Pilgrimage Shots form will accept your submission.

"If you arrive in Kanda and discover you cannot recreate the frame because you never confirmed the angle, you have wasted the excursion. Do that work now."

**Part B: Pilgrimage Storyboard**

Storyboard the shots you plan to capture at your Kanda location:

- The recreation shot matching the specific *Reze Arc* frame
- The establishing shot proving you are at the real location
- At least two support shots: geography, texture, or human rhythm at the location

For each panel: shot type, angle, subject position, any camera movement. Arrows and labeled boxes are sufficient.

Post the floor plan and storyboard to the Stream. Submit the confirmed Google Maps pin before the class period ends.

### 5/19: Geometry Scene Design

Before Quiz 3. Screen the *Tokyo Drift* standoff: the scene where DK and Han face Sean, Neela, and DK's crew in the parking structure. Watch it twice.

Draw the scene from above. Draw the dividing line between DK's side and Sean's side. Name the triangle each group forms: base-down (stable, authoritative) or inverted (precarious, threatened). Label both.

Then assign camera angles for two beats: the moment the standoff opens and the moment tension peaks. High (small, submissive), low (dominant, threatening), eye-level (neutral), or Dutch/canted (instability, panic). For each beat, write one sentence explaining why that angle fits what the scene needs at that moment.

Then: sketch the opening geometry of one dialogue scene from your own film. Where are your subjects? What triangle do they form? Assign one opening angle and write one sentence explaining what it communicates. This sketch goes into your Geometry Worksheet add-on.

Post the floor plan sketch, the two angle assignments, and the Scene Project sketch to the Stream.

## Production Phase

### 5/20: Visual Foreshadowing: Plant/Pattern/Payoff

No quiz today. Three clips from *Bullet Train*, in order.

First: the scene where Lemon introduces his Thomas the Tank Engine system to The Son on the train, assigns Gordon to Tangerine and Percy to The Son. Watch once.

Second: the scene where Lemon slaps a Devious Diesel sticker on Prince's back as she walks away. No dialogue. Watch once.

Third: the scene where Prince tells Tangerine that "the other man kept talking about Thomas the trains" and Tangerine corrects her: "Tank engine." Watch to the moment Tangerine names her as the diesel.

Write one sentence on the Plant: why does the Percy/Gordon scene read as a character quirk rather than a prediction?

Write one sentence on the Pattern: what does the sticker on Prince's back do that dialogue cannot?

Write one sentence on the Payoff: what does Prince's mistake about "Thomas the trains" confirm, and what does a viewer who ignored the sticker not understand about why Tangerine moves when he does?

Then: name one visual detail you will plant in your own film that does work the dialogue is not doing. One sentence on what it is. One sentence on what it pays off. This goes into your Visual Foreshadowing written analysis as a production note.

Post all four parts to the Stream.

### 5/21: Sound Layer Map

Before or after Quiz 4. Watch three minutes of *Ramen Heads* (Koki Shigeno), the soup preparation sequence. Watch once with sound, once muted.

On the muted pass, list every sound that should be present. Label each one:

- D: Diegetic
- ND: Non-diegetic
- F: Foley (liquid, metal, steam, knife contact)
- ADR: Narration or dialogue recorded after filming
- RT: Room tone (the specific ambient sound of that kitchen)

Identify the three most important sounds and explain why each matters in one sentence.

Then answer: What does the sound design tell us about the chef's relationship to this process that the image alone does not? One or two sentences.

Finally: Write a sound plan for your Favorite Place location. Name the three most important sounds you expect to capture there (D, F, or RT), and one sentence on how each one will communicate something about the place. This goes in your Favorite Place add-on form under the audio strategy section.

After the activity, show the Location Sound Log worksheet for two minutes. "Temple bell fading into cicadas with distant train, not ambient sounds." That precision starts now.

Post the labeled list, the three justifications, the Ramen Heads question response, and the Favorite Place sound plan.

## Post-Production Phase

### 5/25: Ramen Heads Editing Rhythm + Kuleshov Effect

No quiz today.

**Activity A: Ramen Heads Editing Rhythm**

Watch the soup preparation sequence in *Ramen Heads* a second time, sound on. You are looking for four things and you will write down a timestamp for each.

**Observational pacing.** Find a stretch where Tomita does one task and the camera holds. Cuts are infrequent. The work unfolds at roughly its real pace. Note the timestamp range and write one sentence on what is unfolding and why the editor lets it sit.

**Process compression.** Find a stretch where the editor cuts fast through many close-up inserts, so twelve hours of broth become thirty seconds of screen time. Note the timestamp range and write one sentence on what got elided.

**Sound bridges.** Find two cuts where sound holds the edit together: a J-cut (the next shot's audio starts before the picture changes) or an L-cut (the previous shot's audio continues over the new image). Boil, knife, ladle, slurp. One sentence each on what the sound bridge is doing.

**Match on action.** Find one cut that hides because motion crosses it: knife meeting board, tongs meeting noodle, ladle entering broth. Timestamp and describe the motion.

Calculate the Average Shot Length for one observational 60-second stretch and one compressed 60-second stretch. Total seconds divided by number of cuts. Two numbers.

Of those four (observational pacing, process compression, sound bridges, match on action), which is the hardest for you to pull off in your own Favorite Place edit? Write one sentence answering that. That sentence is the thesis of your Rhythm of Process written analysis.

Post the four timestamp findings with descriptions, both ASL figures, and the thesis sentence.

**Activity B: Kuleshov Effect Analysis**

Film: *Akira* (Katsuhiro Otomo, 1988), Tetsuo's nightmare and hallucination sequence.

Break the sequence into at least eight distinct cuts. For each:

- What you see: framing, subject, movement
- What came immediately before: the previous shot's emotional tone or content
- What meaning the juxtaposition produces, not just what the shot contains but what placing it after the previous shot generates

Final synthesis in two or three sentences: What do these edits reveal about Tetsuo's mental deterioration that dialogue or performance alone could not?

Before students start: "The third column is the analysis. If it just describes the cut again, you have described two shots, not juxtaposition. What does A before B produce that B alone would not? That is the question."

Tulip Collier's 2025 breakdown is a useful model to share: "Tetsuo and Kaneda drawing on pavement, before: Tetsuo being handed away, meaning: moment of innocence with Kaneda vs. past neglect." That three-part structure is the standard.

Post the eight-cut breakdown and the synthesis paragraph.

### 5/26: Sound Without Words

Before Quiz 5. Film: *Tsugakirei*, Episode 8, from 14:20 to 17:32. No dialogue.

Watch twice: once with sound, once muted.

Answer all four questions in one or two sentences each:

1. What story elements remain fully clear without language? What does the action alone communicate?
2. How do sound design and score communicate tenderness, distance, or urgency here?
3. What emotional information is preserved or enhanced by silence, ambient sound, or underscore alone?
4. Would the scene still land without language? Why?

Run a brief share-out on question 4 before the quiz. Victoria's 2025 response is the target: "We don't need words to feel the butterflies between them. Through action, the boy spends all of his money to make the girl happy."

Then: Identify one scene in your own film where you intend to let sound carry what the dialogue cannot, or where you will use silence deliberately. One sentence describing the moment and what the silence or ambient sound will communicate. This goes in your Tone Flip planning.

Post responses to all four questions and the sentence about your own film.

### 5/27: Rewrite the Soundscape

Production in an open work period or outside class. Video posted by end of day.

Choose any 15-second section from any film. Create an alternative sound design: replace the music with something genre-unexpected (jazz over a tense confrontation, silence over action, ambient rain over domestic calm), and exaggerate one Foley source (breathing, footsteps, steam, metal) while reducing everything else.

Write a justification of three or four sentences: What is gained? What is lost? How does this change what the audience interprets?

"Someone in 2025 took the *Spirited Away* scene where Chihiro discovers her parents have turned into pigs and made it genuinely horrifying by replacing the score with something that belonged in a different film. Another student took a Glee classroom scene and made the teacher feel dangerous. A third stripped everything to ambience alone. All three worked because the justification explained precisely what changed: not 'I added scary music' but 'the scene now communicates dread instead of wonder because the only sound the audience hears is breathing accelerating.' That is the standard."

Post the video file and the written justification.
