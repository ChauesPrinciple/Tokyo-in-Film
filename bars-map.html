Tokyo After Dark

Across Tokyo, 30 late-night places: Ginza counters where the ice is carved by hand, an Ebisu basement that calls itself a coffee shop, a Shimokitazawa jazz room named after a Mingus record, and the bar from Lost in Translation. Tell the bartender what you're in the mood for and let them build it.

1

Apollo Bar Ginza

アポロバー銀座

Chuo ward, Tokyo · Cocktail bar · B1F

I went down to this basement alone and ended up spending hours with the bartender on samurai films, whiskey, and music. By now he's an old friend: a man who loves old movies and, as far as I'm concerned, a modern-day samurai. The room is dim and New York in style, the tonic is house-made, and the whiskey shelf is worth the trip by itself. Expect a ¥1,000 cover and very little English. It's a bar you walk into alone, and it doesn't leave you that way.

Station
Shimbashi (JR / Tokyo Metro), 5 min walk

B1F MEIKO Building, 8-2-15 Ginza, Chuo-ku, Tokyo

Star Bar Ginza

スターバー銀座

Chuo ward, Tokyo · Classic cocktail bar · B1F

Hisashi Kishi was the first Japanese bartender to win the IBA world cocktail championship, and the government has named him a master craftsman. His Ginza basement is hushed and low-lit, the Sidecar is the drink he's known for, and the ice is cut by hand until it nearly disappears. The counter suits a solo visit as well as a celebration. Table charge ¥1,100; plan on ¥6,000 or more. Open 5:00 to 11:30 nightly.

The Pizza Bar On 38th

ザ ピザバー オン 38th

Chuo ward, Tokyo · Hotel bar · high-end · 38F

Book this like a sushi counter, not a bar. Eight seats face a wood-fired oven on the 38th floor of the Mandarin Oriental, reservations open two months out and go in minutes, and once a booking is confirmed you can't change or cancel it. Plan on ¥20,000 to ¥30,000 a person with service and tax. The 48-hour dough keeps a full pizza course from sitting heavy, and on a clear evening Fuji is in the window. It was ranked the second-best pizzeria in the world in 2025. Smart casual.

ShinjukuLate evening: hotel bars, speakeasies, Golden Gai

6

New York Bar

ニューヨークバー
Park Hyatt Tokyo

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Hotel bar · high-end

The Park Hyatt spent nineteen months renovating, reopened in December 2025, and had the sense to leave this bar alone: same fifty-second floor, same windows, same live jazz, with better acoustics and a cocktail list that nods to Lost in Translation, the film where two strangers meet at this bar. Cocktails start at ¥2,000. The ¥2,500 jazz cover begins with the music, so arrive between 5:00 and 6:30 if you'd rather not pay it. Go early on a weeknight for a window seat.

Bar Cocktailbook Shinjuku

バー カクテルブック 新宿

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Cocktail bar · 2F

Akira holds cocktail competition titles, put in twelve years at a Shinjuku hotel bar, and opened this second-floor room in 2011. The menu follows the season and changes monthly, and some of his originals never got names. One reviewer was poured a taste of each spirit alongside the drink, which is the kind of detail people keep mentioning. Thirteen seats, ¥600 charge, closed Sundays and Mondays, near Seibu-Shinjuku.

Bar rest

歌舞伎町 rest
スナックバー rest

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Snack bar · 3F

I couldn't find a single independent review, only the bar's own description, so consider this a lead. Third floor, a minute from Seibu-Shinjuku. Seven counter seats line the window and look straight onto the Seibu and JR tracks, where a train passes about once a minute until the last one, and then the maintenance cars come out. SAKE MARKET Shinjuku

サケマーケット新宿

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Sake bar · 6F

No bartender and no pressure. You pay first, ¥2,700 for ninety minutes or ¥3,800 with no limit, grab three cups, and help yourself from fridges stocked with more than 150 sakes, fruit liqueurs, and shochu. Online booking only, cashless, and outside food is fine. It works for a solo drinker and for a group, and it's an easy place to learn what you like before you sit at a counter where the bartender expects you to know.

Shinjuku (JR / Tokyo Metro / Odakyu / Keio), 3 min walk

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Golden Gai bars

Three bars in Shinjuku Golden Gai, the dense cluster of 200-plus tiny drinking dens off Kabukicho. Happy (Rockin' Soul Bar) is up on the 3rd floor of the Maruha Building at 1-1-7, a soul-and-rock counter. BAR COO is at street level on Golden Gai 3rd street at 1-1-8, five or six seats with Miya behind the counter in fluent English. OPEN BOOK is on Golden Gai 5th street at 1-1-6, no signboard, a lemon sour bar opened in March 2016. Pick any of the three, or bar-hop between them.

Station
Shinjuku (JR / Tokyo Metro / Toei), 5 min walk
Address

Happy (Rockin' Soul Bar)

ハッピー · ロッキンソールバー

3F · Maruha Building

3F Maruha Building, 1-1-7 Kabukicho, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo

Climb to the third floor of the Maruha Building for rock and soul from the sixties through the eighties, played at full volume, with a wall of vinyl you're welcome to pick from. The cover is about ¥900, there are more than a hundred bottles, and the last Friday of each month is disco night.

1F, 1-1-8 Kabukicho, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo

Five or six seats at street level on Golden Gai's third lane, with Miya running things in fluent English. Snacks are whatever she's made: miso soup, seaweed salad, a slice of Gouda. You end up talking with regulars and other travelers, which makes it a good bar to walk into alone. Get there early. Closed Sundays.

OPEN BOOK

オープンブック

Ground floor

1-1-6 Kabukicho, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo

No signboard, just a wooden board that slides open on Golden Gai's fifth lane. You come for the lemon sour: brown-sugar shochu, house lime syrup, Hiroshima lemons, big ice. The books on the shelves belonged to the owner's grandfather, the Naoki Prize-winning novelist Tanaka Komimasa. About fifteen people fit, mostly standing, and it's always full. ¥300 charge, 8:00 to midnight.

No Room For Squares

ノー・ルーム・フォー・スクエアーズ

Setagaya ward, Tokyo · Jazz bar · 4F

Named after the Hank Mobley album. It's on a fourth floor in Shimokitazawa, and you enter through the front of an old Coca-Cola vending machine. 22

BAR &UNDER

バー アンドアンダー

Chiyoda ward, Tokyo · Bar · B1F

A basement bar in Sotokanda, about seven minutes from Akihabara. Koji Yamamoto quit TV production to tend bar and has since come up with more than two hundred sake cocktails, many based on characters, plus collaboration nights with anime and YouTubers every couple of weeks. He says the ampersand in the name means doing something together. About thirty seats, a young crowd, board games, a ¥700 seat charge, and a one-drink minimum; the whole idea is to make bars less intimidating for newcomers. Closes at 11:00, closed Mondays.

バー ウィローズ

Taito ward, Tokyo · Bar · 6F-E

I don't visit Tokyo without coming here, usually several times a trip. Yanagisawa owns the bar, ran the bar at the Yokohama Royal Park Hotel before this, and is about as attentive as a bartender gets; his English is excellent. Before you go

  • Lost in Translation 2003

    Directed by Sofia Coppola

    The New York Bar on the 52nd floor of the Park Hyatt is where Bob and Charlotte meet and drink through the night. It is on this map, and you can walk in and order what they had. Watch it before you go. The film will tell you what the room feels like at 2 a.m. better than any description here.

  • After Dark Novel, 2007

    Haruki Murakami (trans. Jay Rubin)

    A novel that lives entirely between midnight and dawn in Tokyo: a love hotel, a Denny's, a back-office bar, a musician playing a trombone at 4 a.m. Quoted at the top of this page. Read it on the train out and the city will look different when you surface.
