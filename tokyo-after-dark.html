Tokyo After Dark

23 places, in the order I would drink them. Ginza counters early, where the ice is carved by hand. Shinjuku's hidden floors once it gets late. Then south, to a basement in Ebisu that calls itself a coffee shop and a Shimokitazawa jazz room named after a Hank Mobley record. The east side after hours. It ends at a door you have to be let into. Every stop lists its floor, because in Tokyo the floor is the address. Tell the bartender what you are in the mood for and let him build it.

GinzaEarly evening: cocktails and counters

1

Apollo Bar Ginza

アポロバー銀座

Chuo ward, Tokyo · Cocktail bar · B1F

I went down to this basement alone and ended up spending hours with the bartender on samurai films, whiskey, and music. By now he's an old friend: a man who loves old movies and, as far as I'm concerned, a modern-day samurai. The room is dim and New York in style, the tonic is house-made, and the whiskey shelf is worth the trip by itself. Expect a ¥1,000 cover and very little English. Closed Sundays and holidays. It's a bar you walk into alone, and it doesn't leave you that way.

Shimbashi (JR / Tokyo Metro), 5 min walk

B1F MEIKO Building, 8-2-15 Ginza, Chuo-ku, Tokyo

スターバー銀座

2

Star Bar Ginza

スターバー銀座

Chuo ward, Tokyo · Classic cocktail bar · B1F

The ice was the first thing I noticed. It's cut by hand with knives made from Japanese sword steel until it nearly disappears in the glass. I ordered the Sidecar, because that's Hisashi Kishi's drink: brandy, curaçao and lemon, shaken hard and poured over a single hand-cut cube. Kishi holds a world title from the International Bartenders Association and a Medal of Honor from the Emperor. Dim room, nineteen seats, no smoking. ¥1,100 charge, and plan on ¥6,000 or more. Open 5:00 to 11:30 every night. Friends can take a table; I'd sit at the counter alone and watch.

Ginza (Tokyo Metro Ginza/Marunouchi/Hibiya), 3 min walk
Address
B1F MODERNS GINZA, 1-5-13 Ginza, Chuo-ku, Tokyo

Tir na nOg Ginza

3

Tír na nÓg

Tir na nOg Ginza

Chuo ward, Tokyo · Cocktail bar · B1F

Tír na nÓg is the Irish land of eternal youth, and this Ginza basement takes the name seriously: an iron sprite, a key hanging from the ceiling, butterflies, vials of colored fairy dust, gargoyles watching over the room. The cocktails are just as theatrical, some made with cotton candy, with names like the Fairytail Mule and Old Man Milin's Sour. It's a café until 5:00 with a ¥500 charge, then a bar until 4:00 in the morning with a ¥1,000 charge, tax included. Plan on ¥2,000 to ¥6,000, three minutes from Higashi-ginza. I'd bring somebody.

Station
Higashi-ginza (Tokyo Metro Hibiya / Toei Asakusa), 3 min walk
Address
B1F Cheers Ginza, 5-9-5 Ginza, Chuo-ku, Tokyo 104-0061

ShinjukuLate evening: hotel bars, speakeasies, Golden Gai

ニューヨークバー

4

New York Bar

ニューヨークバー
Park Hyatt Tokyo

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Hotel bar · high-end · 52F

Fifty-two floors up in the Park Hyatt, this is the bar from Lost in Translation, where two strangers who came alone find each other. The hotel closed for nineteen months and reopened in December 2025 with the windows and the live jazz where they were. Get there between 5:00 and 6:30 and there's no cover. After that it's ¥3,300 a person, and the first set starts at 6:30. No reservations, and no shorts, sandals or sleeveless shirts. Open until midnight. I'd go early on a weeknight, alone, and take a window seat.

Station
Shinjuku (JR / Tokyo Metro / Odakyu / Keio), 10 min walk
Address
52F Park Hyatt Tokyo, 3-7-1-2 Nishishinjuku, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo

5

Bar Cocktailbook Shinjuku

バー カクテルブック 新宿

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Cocktail bar · 2F

Akira has run this second-floor bar north of Shinjuku Station since 2011, after twelve years at a Shinjuku hotel bar, and his competition awards are on display. I got a warm towel when I sat down. There's no fixed list: he asks what you like and works from the season. When he made mine, he set a small pour of each spirit beside the glass so I could taste them on their own first. The matcha Old Fashioned is worth ordering. There are owls everywhere, because his wife loves them. About a dozen seats, ¥600 charge, closed Sundays and Mondays. A calm counter to sit at alone.

かくれたい

6

KAKURETAI

かくれたい
KAKURETAI Speakeasy Bar

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Speakeasy · B1F

A basement in Arakicho done up with mice and cheese. Yuta is behind the bar and treats you like a regular the first time you walk in. He makes seasonal fruit cocktails, pours Japanese whisky, and has an original watermelon cocktail. There's sofa seating, a designated smoking area, and a DJ every Friday. It's LGBTQ-friendly. Weeknights only, 8:00 to 1:00. It's small and fills up, so reserve. I'd come alone; nobody stays a stranger here long.

Yotsuya-Sanchome (Tokyo Metro Marunouchi), 2 min walk

坊主バー

7

Vowz Bar

坊主バー
Bouzu Bar
Monk Bar

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Monk bar · 2F

The monks behind this second-floor counter in Arakicho are Jodo Shinshu priests, with monks from other sects on staff, and they've been pouring here since 2000. Every night there's a short service and a sermon. The cocktails are named for Buddhist heavens and hells: Gokuraku Jodo is the Pure Land, Aiyoku Jigoku is the hell of lust. The food is vegetarian, things like mock eel and miso-cured tofu. Ask, and a monk will sit with you at your table or give you advice on your life from across the counter. Plan on ¥2,000 to ¥5,000. Open 7:00 to 1:00, closed Sundays and holidays, three minutes from Yotsuya-sanchome. Come alone; you'll have someone to talk to.

Station
Yotsuya-sanchome (Tokyo Metro Marunouchi), Exit 4, 3 min walk

a BAR Sleeping Tales

ア バー スリーピング テイルズ

Shinjuku ward, Tokyo · Cocktail bar · B1F

There's no menu. Toshi asked what I liked, looked at what was in season, and built something around it. The drink people ask for by name is the peanut-butter Old Fashioned. The basement is done up like medieval Europe, and it's counter seats only, so I watched every move. Take the right-hand door at B1F, near Shinjuku Gyoemmae Station. Open from 6:00 (5:00 on weekends) until 2:00, and until 3:00 on Fridays and Saturdays. Closed Wednesdays; book online. A good counter to sit at alone and let someone else decide.

Golden Gai is about two hundred tiny bars packed into the lanes off Kabukicho, and these are the three I'd send you to. 1-1-6 Kabukicho, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo

Kai Tanaka's grandfather was Komimasa Tanaka, the Naoki Prize winner who drank in Golden Gai nearly every day, and his books go up to the ceiling. 10

JANAI COFFEE

ジャナイコーヒー

Shibuya ward, Tokyo · Speakeasy · B1F

Getting in is half the night. I had to solve a riddle on their website just to reach the booking page, and reservations are online only. Down the stairs it looks like a coffee stand; show them the code and a bookcase swings open onto a dark bar. Half the cocktails have coffee in them and half don't, ¥1,400 to ¥1,600 each or ¥3,500 for a course of three. The staff speak English, the playlist is R&B, and they hand you a decaf on the way out. Twenty-eight seats, no smoking, 6:00 to midnight every day. Bring people; the riddle goes faster with help.

Station
Ebisu (JR Yamanote / Tokyo Metro Hibiya), 3 min walk
Address
B1F Yamanen Building, 2-3-13 Ebisuminami, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo

エーテン

Shibuya ward, Tokyo · Record bar · B1F

Across from Ebisu Park there's a row of coin lockers. Pull on one and the whole wall swings open onto a stair down to the basement. Down there it's R&B, soul and jazz on vinyl through top-of-the-line sound, records up to the ceiling, and whoever is on the decks has been incredible every time I've gone; the DJ is the reason I keep coming back. There are twenty original cocktails, ¥1,430 to ¥1,980, plus a ¥1,100 seat charge. Thirty seats, open every night from 7:00 to 3:00; book ahead for weekends. Bring someone who hasn't seen the lockers open.

Never Never Land

ネバーネバーランド
ネヴァーネヴァーランド
下北沢ネバーネバーランド

Setagaya ward, Tokyo · Izakaya · music bar · 2F

Never Never Land has been going in Shimokitazawa since 1978, on the second floor of Yoshino Heights, near Studio Noah. It moved here in the mid-2000s when its first building across the street came down. The walls are a patchwork of photos, fliers and posters for concerts and underground theater, and gigs happen now and then. The kitchen does Burmese-style curry and other spiced dishes, and the room is non-smoking. Plan on ¥2,000 to ¥3,000. Open 6:00 to 2:00 every night. Regulars and newcomers end up at the same tables; I'd go with friends.

No Room For Squares

ノー・ルーム・フォー・スクエアーズ

Setagaya ward, Tokyo · Jazz bar · 4F

Named for the 1964 Hank Mobley album on Blue Note. You get in through the front of an old Coca-Cola vending machine on a fourth floor in Shimokitazawa. Inside are more than a thousand jazz records and no menu: you tell the owner what you like and the owner mixes it, mocktails included, with Ichiro's whisky on the shelf. Some nights there's live jazz. Twenty-two seats, cash only, no smoking before 6:00. Open 2:00 p.m. to 2:00 a.m., days off vary. A good first bar to walk into nervous; I'd send you in alone.

バー サワ

14

Bar sawa

バー サワ

Meguro ward, Tokyo · Whisky bar · 1F

Shigeko Shintani opened this Nakameguro whisky bar in 1985, and she and her staff have visited more than a hundred distilleries. There are over eight hundred whiskies behind the counter, including a 52-year-old Dalmore Sirius, one of twelve in the world, and Macallans from 1948 and 1951. Ten seats inside, more on the terrace, smoking allowed. ¥800 charge, half-price drinks from 5:00 to 8:00, open every night until 5:00 in the morning. Look for the yellow light over the door. I'd bring somebody who loves whisky and plan to spend more than I meant to.

Station
Nakameguro (Tokyo Metro Hibiya), 5 min walk
Address
1F Forum Nakameguro, 1-6-7 Higashiyama, Meguro-ku, Tokyo

17

BAR &UNDER

バー アンドアンダー

Chiyoda ward, Tokyo · Sake cocktail bar · B1F

A basement in Sotokanda managed by Koji Yamamoto, who worked his way from assistant director to director on TV variety shows before he started tending bar. He has created more than two hundred sake cocktails themed on characters, and about every two weeks there's a collaboration night with an anime or a YouTuber. He says the ampersand in the name means doing something together. About thirty seats, a crowd mostly in their twenties to forties, a ¥700 seat charge and a one-drink minimum. Open 6:00 to 11:00 on weekdays, from 4:00 on weekends and holidays, closed Mondays. Bring friends; that's the point of the name.

ジャズハウスナル
Jazz House Naru

Chiyoda ward, Tokyo · Jazz livehouse · B1F

NARU has been open since 1969, a minute from the Bridge exit at Ochanomizu. 19

Game Bar A-Button

ゲームバー エーボタン

Taito ward, Tokyo · Game bar · 1F

Nagamine managed Sega arcades before he put his severance into this bar in 2008, in a former ramen shop east of Akihabara. It's packed with his own collection: consoles, controllers, figures, merchandise. Don't come expecting to play everything. After Japan's software copyright association started sending letters to game bars, he stopped keeping some makers' machines playable. Thirteen seats, nine at the counter. I'd go with someone who grew up on the same consoles.

Inaricho (Tokyo Metro Ginza), 4 min walk

20

BAR Willow's

バー ウィローズ

Taito ward, Tokyo · Whisky & cocktail bar · 6F-E

I don't visit Tokyo without coming here, usually several times a trip. Yanagisawa owns the bar and was chief bartender at the Yokohama Royal Park Hotel before this. He's about as attentive as a bartender gets, and his English is excellent. The ice is hand-crafted and priced like it, and that is what good service costs. Every chair is an antique, picked one at a time, and the chair in the logo means there's always a seat waiting for you. After enough trips, there is. Over two hundred whiskies, good alcohol-free cocktails, fresh coffee. Sixteen seats, groups of four at most. Tuesday to Saturday until 3:00, Sundays until 11:00, closed Mondays and holidays.

映画館ジャズ

22

Eigakan Jazz

映画館ジャズ

Bunkyo ward, Tokyo · Jazz bar · B1F

Eigakan means cinema. Masahiro Yoshida started it in the seventies to screen 16mm films, Oshima, Imamura and the rest of the Japanese New Wave. When the old building came down, he moved to Hakusan and turned it over to jazz. He built the tube amplifiers and the speakers himself, the records turn on a sixties Denon and an eighties Micro, and there's a Hiroshima Mon Amour poster on the wall. It's free jazz, played loud. Twenty seats, cash only, smoking allowed, no reservations, ¥1,000 to ¥2,000 for the night. Tuesday to Saturday, 4:00 to 10:00, a minute from Hakusan Station. Come alone and listen.

Station
Hakusan (Toei Mita Line A3), 1 min walk

SAKEBARO Aoyama

酒バー O
Sakebaro
SAKEBARO 青山店

Minato ward, Tokyo · Exclusive sake club · 1F

Members only, with screening to get in. Inside are a tin-cube counter, a tin piano that plays itself, LED walls that move from blue to red, and staff who can give you the brewery, yeast, and polish rate of every pour. Conversation stays hushed. Now and then there's a ticketed dinner for non-members; book online and they email you the address. You can walk into everything else in this guide. Not this one.

Reservation or membership required. Arrange access well before you travel.

Before you go

  • Lost in Translation 2003

    Directed by Sofia Coppola

    The New York Bar on the 52nd floor of the Park Hyatt is where Bob and Charlotte meet and drink through the night. It is on this map, and you can walk in and order what they had. Watch it before you go. The film will tell you what the room feels like at 2 a.m. better than any description here.

  • After Dark Novel, 2007

    Haruki Murakami (trans. Jay Rubin)

    A novel that lives entirely between midnight and dawn in Tokyo: a love hotel, a Denny's, a back-office bar, a musician playing a trombone at 4 a.m. Quoted at the top of this page. Read it on the train out and the city will look different when you surface.
