# Reference: Ringu and J-Horror

Checked facts for any page, assignment, or analysis that discusses *Ringu* (Nakata Hideo, 1998) or Japanese horror. Checked October 28, 2026 against Wikipedia, the Japanese Film Database, Jump Cut, and producer interviews. Use the confirmed facts freely. Do not repeat the corrected claims. Treat the unverified items as unverified: leave them out or ask Robert Ladd.

## Ringu (1998): confirmed

- Released in Japan on January 31, 1998, distributed by Toho, on a double bill with *Rasen* (*Spiral*, dir. Iida Jōji), shot at the same time.
- Made by the Ring/Spiral Production Committee. Producers: Shinya Kawai, Taka Ichise, Takenori Sento.
- Budget: US$1.5 million.
- Production: nine months and one week in total; three or four months of pre-production and script work; a five-week shoot.
- Japanese box office: about ¥1.7 billion (about US$13 million); distribution income ¥1 billion. Worldwide gross: about US$19.5 million.
- Based on Suzuki Kōji's 1991 novel *Ring*. An earlier TV adaptation, *Ring: Kanzenban* (1995, dir. Takigawa Chisui), is closer to the novel.
- Nakata's previous feature was *Joyū-rei* (*Don't Look Up*, 1996).
- Leads: Matsushima Nanako (Asakawa Reiko), Sanada Hiroyuki (Takayama Ryūji). Composer: Kawai Kenji (also *Ghost in the Shell*, 1995).
- Sadako is played by Inō Rie. Her movement was filmed with her walking backwards in a disjointed way, and the footage was then reversed.
- *Ring 2* (January 23, 1999) grossed ¥3.57 billion in Japan, more than double *Ringu*.
- Kurosawa Kiyoshi's *Cure* opened in Japan in late December 1997, about a month before *Ringu*.

## Remakes and the boom: confirmed

- *The Ring* (dir. Gore Verbinski, October 2002): $48 million budget, about $250 million worldwide, rated PG-13, Naomi Watts, score by Hans Zimmer. *The Ring Two* (2005) was directed by Nakata. *Rings* (2017).
- *Ju-on*: began with two shorts, "Katasumi" and "4444444444", shown in the TV film *Gakkō no Kaidan G* (1998). Shimizu Takashi made them while studying under Kurosawa Kiyoshi at the Film School of Tokyo. Two direct-to-video films followed in 2000, shot in nine days each, and they became hits by word of mouth.
- *Ju-on: The Grudge* opened in Japanese theatres on January 25, 2003. Its earlier October 2002 date is a festival premiere. Production companies include Nikkatsu, Oz, and Xanadeux. Shimizu's ghost concept drew on a butoh group that painted their bodies white.
- *The Grudge* (2004, dir. Shimizu): $10 million budget, about $187 million worldwide. American entries: *The Grudge* (2004), *The Grudge 2* (2006), *The Grudge 3* (2009), *The Grudge* (US theatrical release January 3, 2020).
- *Gakkō no Kaidan*: books by schoolteacher Tsunemitsu Tōru, who collected ghost stories from students (first volume 1990). Kansai TV aired a six-episode miniseries in 1994. Film version 1995 (dir. Hirayama Hideyuki).
- Tartan Asia Extreme launched in 2001. Hamish McAlpine said he watched *Ringu* and *Audition* back to back at the end of 1999 and was "totally blown away by them."
- *Audition* (Miike Takashi, 1999), from Murakami Ryū's 1997 novel.

## Corrected claims: do not repeat

- Lady Rokujō (*The Tale of Genji*) is not an *ikiryō* "even in death." An *ikiryō* is a living spirit. She possesses Aoi while alive and returns after death as a *shiryō*.
- Izanagi and Izanami do not descend into Yomi together. Izanami dies and goes to Yomi; Izanagi follows her.
- Nakagawa Nobuo's *Yotsuya Kaidan* (1959) was not the first film of the play, which had been filmed many times before. It is the most influential version. Oiwa's defining look is her disfigured face and her hair falling out; the white robe and loose black hair are general *yūrei* iconography.
- Inō Rie's troupe from 1987 was Banyū Inryoku, an avant-garde theatre company, which some sources call kabuki. Do not call her "kabuki-trained" without that qualification; traditional kabuki is performed by men.
- No source supports a butoh influence on Sadako's movement. The documented butoh link is Shimizu's, for *Ju-on*.
- *Ju-on* is a haunted-house curse story, not a school story.
- *Ju-on: The Grudge* was not released at the same time in ten territories; releases were staggered.
- The 2020 *Grudge* is not a 2019 film.
- *Book of Shadows: Blair Witch 2* (2000) was a critical failure and a commercial disappointment, but it earned more than its budget. Do not call it a flop.
- "J-horror" was not an established label in 1991 or 1994. Applying it to *Scary True Stories* (1991) or the 1994 *Gakkō no Kaidan* series is retrospective.
- "Toho approached Nakata" simplifies the history: a production committee made the film and Toho distributed it.
- The composer is Kawai Kenji (Kenji Kawai), not "Kinji."

## Unverified: leave out or ask

- That the phone ring was mixed from four phone sounds, and Nakata's quoted line about Hollywood phones.
- Nakata's account of visiting cinemas and watching teenagers phone their friends (attributed to a 2000 *Offscreen* interview).
- That Omega Project was the "first to replicate Toho's formula" with *Audition*. Whether Omega Project was itself one of *Ringu*'s production partners was not confirmed.
- That a publisher backed out of *Ju-on* after the 2000 video films, and that the 2003 film was shot in one week.
